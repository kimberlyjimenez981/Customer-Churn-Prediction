# Customer-Churn-Prediction


📉 Customer Churn Prediction

Business Challenge:

Customer churn represents a significant and ongoing risk to revenue. Without a predictive approach, the business has limited visibility into which customers are likely to leave and when. This lack of foresight makes retention efforts reactive rather than strategic. Churn is especially costly because acquiring new customers can cost between five and twenty-five times as much as retaining existing ones, meaning every lost customer represents both immediate revenue loss and sunk acquisition costs.

Solution Overview:

This project delivers a machine learning–based churn prediction system designed to identify customers at risk of leaving before churn occurs. By acting as an early warning system, the model enables proactive intervention through targeted retention strategies. This approach allows the business to shift from reacting to churn after it happens to preventing it before revenue is lost.


Methodology:

Historical data from 5,000 customers were analyzed to understand behavioral and contractual patterns associated with churn. The dataset included information on contract type, customer tenure, monthly charges, payment methods, and service usage. Multiple machine learning models were trained and evaluated to identify the approach that best captured churn behavior. The final model achieved an accuracy of seventy-seven percent, which is considered strong performance for a customer churn prediction problem.

Key Insights:

The analysis revealed several clear drivers of customer churn. Contract structure played a major role, with customers on month-to-month plans being significantly more likely to leave than those on longer-term contracts, suggesting lower commitment and higher price sensitivity. Customer tenure was another critical factor, as a large share of churn occurred within the first six months of a customer’s lifecycle, pointing to gaps in onboarding and early engagement. Perceived value also strongly influenced retention, as customers paying higher prices without receiving additional services such as technical support or online security were substantially more likely to churn, indicating misalignment between pricing and value delivery.

Recommended Actions:

Based on these findings, the project recommends shifting more customers toward longer-term contracts through incentives and added benefits, strengthening the onboarding experience during the early stages of the customer lifecycle, and increasing perceived value by bundling premium services with higher-priced plans. Additionally, optimizing payment methods by encouraging automatic bank transfers or credit card payments can further support retention, as payment behavior showed a measurable relationship with churn.


Expected Business Impact:

By implementing the recommended retention strategies, the business is projected to reduce customer churn by approximately 25% within a single quarter. This improvement is expected to protect an estimated $500,000 to $1 million in annual recurring revenue, depending on customer lifetime value and average contract size. Implementation costs are minimal, as the initiatives primarily involve optimizing existing processes rather than investing in new infrastructure. Initial results should become measurable within the first three months, with the full impact realized over six months.

Conclusion:

This project demonstrates how predictive analytics can transform customer retention into a proactive, data-driven strategy. The churn model not only identifies which customers are most likely to leave, but also explains the underlying reasons behind churn behavior. By pairing predictive insights with clear, actionable recommendations, the system enables the business to protect revenue, improve customer lifetime value, and make more informed retention decisions at scale.
